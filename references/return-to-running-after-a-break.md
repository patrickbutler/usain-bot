# Returning to Running After a Break

Detraining begins within about two weeks of stopping structured running,
and aerobic capacity (VO2max) can drop measurably within three to four
weeks off. Tendons and bone lose load tolerance faster than the
cardiovascular system recovers its fitness memory, which is exactly why
a returning runner can feel "fine" effort-wise while their
musculoskeletal system is still under-prepared for the volume they were
handling before the break.

The practical implication: return to running after a break gap protocol
should never simply resume the prior week's plan. Effort (how hard it
feels) recovers faster than tissue tolerance (what the legs can safely
absorb), so the felt-effort signal is not a reliable guide for how much
volume or how long a run to take on. The safer heuristic is to rebuild
from a reduced volume for at least as many easy sessions as the length
of the break would suggest, holding long runs and quality work until a
short run-walk-run comfort streak is reestablished, then reintroducing
long runs before quality work.

For breaks longer than about six weeks, treat the return as a new
training block rather than a continuation: use the athlete's current
demonstrated capability as the new starting point, not the volume they
were doing before the layoff.
